    Reaction range

    Signal window 0% – 100%
    Reaction 0 – 255
    How it works

    The signal window is stretched onto the reaction range. Above the window the reaction is always at maximum; below it the reaction stays at the minimum, or 0. Example: signal 0–60% → reaction 150–250.

    Curve bends the stretch: LED brightness looks logarithmic (50% → 100% barely changes), so a curve of ×2–×2.5 keeps quiet and mid signals dim and makes the reaction look even. Below linear it lifts quiet signals.

    65 – 180 BPM

    The tempo estimate looks for a beat only in this range. Narrowing it around the music (e.g. 115–135 for house) keeps it from locking onto half or double the tempo.

    Noise

    Details

    Hann-windowed FFT at the selected analysis rate (the top frequency is half that rate), 16 logarithmic bands for the classic preview modes (16/32/64 for the lamp and the lab-preview modes) with a spectral tilt and an optional per-band reference, a level curve with compression or expansion, envelope attack/release, adaptive noise floor, a spectral-flux onset detector per band group and an experimental tempo estimate (may be wrong on complex music).

    Tilt lifts the highs (or the lows) by that many dB per octave from 1 kHz: music loses energy toward the top, so +3…+4.5 dB/oct lets the high bands move as much as the low ones. Band balance normalises each band to its own average of the last few seconds instead of the song's: every band then uses its full range, at the cost of the true balance between them. Bass / Mid / High trim one group by up to ±12 dB. Groups set which bands make up bass, mid and high — their levels, hits and the tempo. Band smoothing blends every band with its neighbors for a calmer spectrum.

    Window shapes each frame before the FFT: Hann is the balanced choice; Blackman-Harris keeps a loud bass from leaking into the bands next to it (wider peaks); flat-top reads tone levels exactly but blurs frequencies; rectangular is the sharpest and leaks the most. Tones read the same level in every window. Peak hold keeps a band at its peak before the release starts. Memory is how long the onset threshold remembers the flux: short adapts quickly to quiet and busy parts, long keeps it steady.

    Orientation and accuracy

    Flipping only changes the display. Addressing is serpentine, like Led::get_index; long strips wrap onto several display lines.

    Effects are hand-ported to C++/WASM; an exact match with the firmware is not proven (FastLED version, frame timing, physical LEDs). LED output simulation is approximate: no current limiting or dithering.
